# Desert arena visual analysis

## Scope

The analysis covers three supplied side-view desert arena screenshots. Only
general visual and gameplay-readability principles are retained. No protected
names, characters, text, props, silhouettes, or exact compositions are reused.

## Shared visual patterns

- Large silhouettes remain simple, while interest comes from asymmetry inside
  each mass: stepped cliffs, narrow necks, caves, arches, leaning vegetation,
  suspended bridges, and isolated landmarks.
- Desert terrain is not one color. It uses light sand, warm beige stone,
  golden faces, terracotta side planes, muted brown recesses, and near-black
  caves. This creates depth without requiring realistic rendering.
- Lighting is organized in large zones. Broad sunlit caps and sand patches sit
  against darker cliff sides and deep entrances; tiny highlights are secondary.
- Saturated accents are sparse and strategic: vegetation, painted props, water,
  cloth, or technology punctuate large neutral terrain masses.
- Props create narrative focal points and scale. They are clustered rather than
  tiled evenly across the whole arena.
- Decorative depth is common but subordinate. Short ledges, crates, masonry
  rims, stairs, and facade details do not form long continuous paths and remain
  weaker than the real gameplay silhouette.

## Readability model

The useful rule is not "remove all 3D cues." It is a hierarchy:

1. Real collision routes use the longest, cleanest, highest-contrast edges.
2. Potential post-destruction surfaces use the same edge language but may begin
   partially occluded by a clearly destructible object.
3. Decorative depth uses short, interrupted, lower-contrast shapes that attach
   visually to a wall or prop and cannot be read as a traversable route.
4. Background or atmospheric shapes use still lower contrast and softer edges.

## Application to the desert sci-fi arena

- Keep the X-only layout, side-facing bunker entrances, bridges, supported
  floating platforms, tactical pockets, and open projectile airspace from v4.
- Restore the natural silhouette rhythm, erosion, shadow pockets, material
  richness, selective small props, and local depth seen in the original master.
- Replace the uniform orange block grid with a small family of related desert
  materials and irregular large rock formations.
- Use light sand on selected real routes, darker terracotta on cliff sides,
  deep cool-brown bunker recesses, and cyan technology for controlled contrast.
- Add sparse original desert vegetation and small technological debris as
  secondary accents; do not turn them into new platforms or repeat any supplied
  reference prop literally.
